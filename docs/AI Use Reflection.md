AI Use Reflection

*Reflection on where AI accelerated the CareerPilot project and where human review remained essential.*  
**AI was most effective as a multiplier for speed and structure; it became risky when its output could be mistaken for evidence or a final product decision.**

**Where AI Was Most Effective**

* **Initial research and exploration.** AI helped me conduct initial research faster, explore competitors, structure research questions and identify useful directions to investigate. It was especially valuable for creating a strong starting point, while the underlying claims still needed verification.  
* **Documentation and synthesis.** AI significantly reduced the time required to draft and refine the PRD, research artefacts, KPI logic, Responsible AI documentation and presentation materials. It also helped turn rough notes into clearer, more consistent product documentation.  
* **Moving information between tools.** One of the largest efficiency gains was transferring approved information from research into the PRD, from the PRD into Jira and Confluence, and from build-readiness decisions into Lovable. This reduced repetitive administrative work and helped keep deliverables aligned.

**Where AI Was Risky**

* **Confident inaccuracies and unsupported assumptions.** AI can present incorrect information with convincing language. During the project, I had to correct competitor assumptions and remove wording that could have looked like a real user quote when it was only a synthetic persona statement. This showed why AI output cannot be treated as evidence by default.  
* **Overgeneralisation and automation bias.** AI summaries can make limited evidence sound stronger than it is and can generate many plausible features or recommendations very quickly. I still had to keep the five-person survey clearly directional, label simulated monitoring data correctly, and decide what belonged in the MVP, what moved to Later, and what should be rejected.  
* **Privacy and sensitive information.** AI makes it easy to process large amounts of personal and professional data, so I treated data minimisation as part of the workflow. Credentials, secrets and unnecessary sensitive information were not intentionally provided to AI tools.

**How I Managed the Risks**

* **Human review remained the final gate.** I reviewed AI-generated research summaries, requirements, roadmap changes and implementation outputs before accepting them. When an output conflicted with evidence, the latest PRD or the actual MVP, I corrected or rejected it.  
* **Sources of truth stayed explicit.** The latest approved product decisions, supporting research and real implementation were used to resolve inconsistencies. Research limitations, simulated data and AI interpretation were kept distinguishable from verified facts.

**Main Learning**

I would use AI again for research support, synthesis, drafting, information transfer, prototyping and implementation because the time savings are substantial. But I would not use it as a substitute for user evidence, source verification or product judgement. The best results came from AI speed combined with human review: AI proposed and structured the work; I verified the evidence, challenged assumptions, chose trade-offs and owned the final decisions.  
**Use AI to accelerate the work; keep evidence, judgement and accountability human-owned.**
